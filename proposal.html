1. Executive summary

MB Musawo is a complete platform for hospitals and clinic networks in Uganda and East Africa. Patients use their phones to see a doctor, call an ambulance, get tests and medicine at home and pay with Mobile Money. Hospital staff — doctors, nurses, laboratory, pharmacy, ambulance team and management — work in one connected system, on any computer or phone.

The working prototype already covers 10 user roles and more than 140 screens, including the full journey from a patient’s symptom to a doctor’s consultation, lab result, prescription, payment and printed receipt. This proposal describes what the system does, how it will be built for production and how a pilot can start.

What we propose

A 6-month programme: production build (Python backend and Next.js app), security and legal review, then an 8-week pilot in one hospital with its laboratory, pharmacy and ambulance service — followed by rollout across branches.

2. The problem

  • Patients queue for hours and travel far just to find out whether a doctor, test or medicine is available.
  • Prices are often unclear until the counter, and paper results get lost, so tests are repeated.
  • Doctors, laboratories, pharmacies and ambulances work in separate systems — or on paper.
  • In emergencies, people do not know which number to call or which hospital can send an ambulance.
  • Managers lack real-time figures on waiting times, stock, beds, claims and revenue across branches.
  • Many systems are built for fast internet and English only, which excludes many patients.

4. What the system does — by user

Patients

  • Talk to a licensed doctor by chat, phone call or video for one fixed price of UGX 35,000, connected to the first available doctor.
  • Send photos (wounds, rashes, medicine boxes, test papers) to the doctor, nurse, pharmacy, laboratory or reception.
  • Request an ambulance from the chosen hospital and follow it live on a map with the driver’s name, number plate and a call button.
  • Guided symptom check in English, Luganda and Kiswahili, with danger signs checked first and advice on urgency, the right doctor and useful tests.
  • Lab tests with price comparison, home sample collection and results shown against normal ranges; forward results to a doctor in one tap.
  • Medicine ordering from licensed pharmacies with delivery, prescription checks and order tracking.
  • Nurse home visits, hospital bookings, queue ticket on the phone and reminders by SMS or WhatsApp.
  • Medical records, consent management (who can see what) and a log of who viewed the record.
  • Home readings (blood pressure, sugar, weight, temperature), medicine reminders, pregnancy tracker, family planning, child immunisation and family accounts.
  • Payments by 17 methods, a wallet, refund requests and a printable receipt for every payment.

Doctors

  • Works for any doctor on duty; each doctor signs in as themselves.
  • Queue of paid app consultations that any free doctor can accept; chat, call or video with the patient.
  • Appointment workflow (requested, confirmed, arrived, in consultation, done) with patient notifications.
  • Patient file with a hospital patient banner, trends for chronic conditions, results and consultation notes that cannot be deleted (corrections only).
  • e-Prescriptions with allergy, interaction and pregnancy checks; lab and imaging requests; referral letters, sick notes and medical certificates.
  • Inpatient list, theatre list, availability, earnings and payout requests.

Nurses

  • Patients waiting with triage priority from vital signs; record vital signs; medicine rounds with “given” records.
  • Ward and bed board, admissions, transfers and discharge support; patient registration.
  • Messages and photos from patients; ambulance arrivals so the emergency bed is ready; home visits.

Laboratory

  • Order queue, sample registration with barcode, sample rejection with reason.
  • Result entry with automatic high, low and critical flags; QA checklist.
  • Quality control with Levey-Jennings chart and Westgard rules; release is blocked when QC fails until corrective action is recorded.
  • Head-of-department sign-off (deputy if the head entered the result), release to patient, corrected reports, test catalogue, equipment and reagents.

Pharmacy

  • Inventory with batch and expiry; expired stock cannot be sold; low-stock alerts.
  • Order pipeline (received, ready, out for delivery, delivered); prescriptions can be filled only once.
  • Barcode scanning, wholesale ordering for verified pharmacies (National Drug Authority) and drug search.

Home care

  • Visit requests, daily schedule, live map, sample collection for the lab, visit records and staff safety alerts.

Ambulance team

  • Each hospital dispatches only its own ambulances.
  • Dispatch board with incoming requests, assignment of a free ambulance, trip status (dispatched, on scene, going to hospital, arrived, completed).
  • Fleet management (plate, type, driver, crew, status), live map and trip history.

Hospital administration

  • Command centre for all branches: clients, waiting time, revenue, family planning output.
  • Patient registration, wards and beds, operating theatre, radiology, blood bank and duty roster.
  • Queue and triage, appointments board, family planning register (couple-years of protection), antenatal risk register, telemedicine.
  • Billing and daily reconciliation, insurance claims, SMS/WhatsApp campaigns, commodity stock and transfers between branches, outreach camps, client feedback, analytics, staff roles and offline notes.

Platform administration

  • Provider approvals with licence checks; countries and emergency numbers that update every patient’s SOS screen.
  • De-identified exports for the Ministry of Health (HMIS / DHIS2), research and partners; privacy and data-subject requests; audit log.
  • Super administrator: single-use invitations (48 hours), system health, roles and permissions, subscriptions and revenue.

7. Emergencies and ambulances

  • A 3-second SOS button sends the patient’s location to their emergency contact; emergency numbers for Uganda, Kenya, Tanzania and Rwanda are always available, even offline.
  • The patient chooses a hospital (nearest first, showing free ambulances). Each hospital dispatches only its own ambulances.
  • The patient sees the ambulance move on a map with arrival time, driver name, number plate and a call button.
  • Nurses see arriving ambulances so a bed is ready; first-aid guides help while waiting.

8. Easy for everyone, on every device

  • Large, simple buttons designed so that even a child can find a doctor or call an ambulance.
  • Works in the browser on iPhone, iPad, Android, Windows, macOS and Linux; can be installed on the home screen like an app; no app store needed.
  • Works on weak networks and offline for emergency numbers, records and notes; changes sync when the connection returns.
  • English, Luganda and Kiswahili; light and dark mode; large-text option.
  • People without smartphones can use USSD (*284*88#) and WhatsApp.

9. Security, privacy and compliance

Health data is sensitive. The system is designed to meet Uganda’s Data Protection and Privacy Act, 2019 and professional regulations.

  • Role-based access: each person sees only what their role needs; the server decides the role.
  • Patients control access to their records; every view is logged and visible to the patient.
  • Two-step verification for all staff; single-use, 48-hour invitations for administrators.
  • Encryption in transit and at rest; field-level encryption for diagnoses and results; no health details in SMS or notifications.
  • Clinical safety rules built in: notes cannot be deleted, results cannot be released before quality control and sign-off, prescriptions are filled once, expired medicine cannot be sold.
  • Append-only audit log, alerts for unusual access, breach-response plan and data-subject request handling.
  • Provider licences verified with the medical council, nursing council, allied health council and National Drug Authority before approval.

12. Current status

A complete clickable prototype exists today. It runs in any modern browser, uses realistic Ugandan sample data and demonstrates every module and flow in this proposal. In the prototype, payments, SMS, video and ambulance dispatch are simulated; they become real when the production backend connects to the payment, messaging and video providers. No real patient data is used.

14. How we will measure success

  • Average waiting time at the hospital (target: below 30 minutes).
  • Share of patients who keep follow-up appointments, and fewer no-shows after reminders.
  • Ambulance response time from request to arrival.
  • Laboratory turnaround time and QC compliance.
  • Days to insurance payment and claim approval rate.
  • Stock-outs of essential medicines and family planning commodities.
  • Patient satisfaction (rating and Net Promoter Score).
